Figures

Profiles of the people who shaped this timeline — from the last Crusader king of Jerusalem to the founder of the Sabirid Dynasty.

Timeline Figures Events Realms
c. 1148 – c. 1213 · Founder, Sabirid Dynasty

Sabira bint Yusef

Sabira bint Yusef (Arabic: صابرة بنت يوسف‎, Ṣābira bint Yūsuf; c. 1148 – c. 1213), often referred to in later Sabirid sources as Sabira the Liberator (al-Muḥarrira), was the founder of the Sabirid dynasty and the first ruler of the state later known as the Jasmine Throne. She came to prominence during the political collapse of the Kingdom of Jerusalem in the 1170s and led the coalition that deposed Baldwin IV in 1178, ending centralized Crusader rule in Jerusalem.

Sabira ruled from 1178 until her death in 1213. During her reign, Jerusalem was transformed from the unstable center of a declining Crusader kingdom into the capital of a growing Levantine state. Her government rebuilt regional trade routes, restored agricultural production after years of famine and unrest, and established a more centralized administrative system that outlasted her dynasty itself.

Later imperial historians portrayed Sabira as a near-legendary liberator and enlightened founder. Modern historians, while skeptical of many later embellishments, generally regard her as a highly capable political organizer and state-builder who benefited from the fragmentation of both the Crusader states and neighboring powers during the late 12th century.

1161 – c. 1179 · Last Crusader King of Jerusalem

Baldwin IV

Baldwin IV (1161 – c. 1179), known in Crusader annals as Baldwin the Leper and in the historiography of the Jasmine Throne as Baldwin the Defeated, was the last Crusader King of Jerusalem, reigning from his coronation in 1174 until his deposition in 1178. His reign witnessed the catastrophic unraveling of Frankish authority in the Levant, culminating in the Jasmine Revolt led by Sabira bint Yusef and the establishment of the Imperial Throne of Jasmine.

His reign is principally studied as the closing chapter of the Latin Kingdom of Jerusalem. Modern scholarship, synthesizing Sabirid and fragmented Latin sources, views Baldwin not as a singularly villainous monarch but as the ill-fated steward of an already fragile feudal state whose structural weaknesses were exposed and accelerated under the pressures of famine, fiscal exhaustion, aristocratic factionalism, and administrative collapse.

1847 – 1906 · Empress of the Jasmine Empire

Amina III

Amina III bint Harun (1 October 1847 – 12 June 1906) was the Empress of the Jasmine Empire from 1878 until her death in 1906. She is remembered as the “Physician-Empress” for her patronage of medicine and science, and for steering the empire through the Revolts of the 1890s, a crisis that nearly shattered Jasmine rule.
